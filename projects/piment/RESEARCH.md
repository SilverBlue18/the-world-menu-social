# Le piment: research and fact-check (video 1)

## Claims we can use (with sources)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Chili (Capsicum annuum) was domesticated in Mexico; wild harvesting ~8,000 years ago, domesticated by ~6,000 years ago | Perry & Flannery, PNAS 2007 (PMC1924538); Kraft et al., PNAS 2014 (UC Davis) |
| Domestication region: central-east Mexico (southern Puebla / northern Oaxaca to Veracruz) | Kraft/Gepts et al., PNAS 2014; UC Davis news |
| Columbus met the chili in the Caribbean (journal, Jan 1493: "aji ... their pepper"), and called it "pepper" (pimiento) though it is unrelated to black pepper | JSTOR Daily; National Geographic; Atlas Obscura; Aramco World |
| The Portuguese were the main spreaders: along the West African coast and to India | JSTOR Daily; YaleGlobal |
| In India the chili arrived with the Portuguese in the early 16th century (Goa, from 1510); before, Indian heat came from black pepper | Britannica ("one good fact"); Dawn/Collingham "Curry"; Time (Collingham) |
| Within ~30 years of Columbus's first voyage, at least three kinds of chili grew in Goa | Time 2007, citing Lizzie Collingham |
| In Goa chilies were called "Pernambuco pepper", after the Brazilian region | JSTOR Daily; YaleGlobal |
| First written record of chili in China: 1591 (Gao Lian); local gazettes reach Sichuan in 1749 | National Geographic (Brian Dott); Nautilus |
| India today: world's largest producer, consumer and exporter of chili | ICAR Indian Horticulture 2023; Spices Board / ICAR-IISR |

## Claims left out (and why)
- **"Korea had no chili before the Japanese invasions of 1592"**: disputed by Korean researchers (Journal of Ethnic Foods 2015/2017); not used.
- **Exact date the Portuguese first planted chili in Goa**: not documented precisely; we only say "early 16th century" / "a few decades".
- **Thailand "revolutionised" by the Portuguese**: only one popular source (Time); not strong enough for a precise claim.
- **"40% of world production"** (ICAR) vs "25%" (other sources): figures disagree, so we say "the world's largest producer" without a percentage.
