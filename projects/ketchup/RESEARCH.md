# Ketchup: research (pilot of the new real-food format)

## Claims used in the script
| # | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Ketchup" comes from the Hokkien (Southern Min) word kê-tsiap, a sauce made from fermented fish, from southern China. | [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/how-was-ketchup-invented), [HISTORY](https://www.history.com/articles/ketchup-surprising-ancient-history) |
| 2 | British traders tasted it in Southeast Asia (Indonesia, the Philippines) around the late 1600s / early 1700s and took the idea home. | NatGeo, HISTORY |
| 3 | 18th-century British ketchups used mushrooms, walnuts, oysters (or anchovies). | NatGeo, HISTORY |
| 4 | The first tomato ketchup recipe dates from 1812, by James Mease, a Philadelphia scientist. | NatGeo, HISTORY |
| 5 | Heinz introduced its tomato ketchup in 1876. | NatGeo, HISTORY |

## Left out (and why)
- "Tomato ketchup still had anchovies in 1817": only one secondary mention found, not confirmed by NatGeo/HISTORY.
- "Ketchup is 2,300 years old (300 BC)": HISTORY speaks of fermented pastes c. 300 BC, which is a precursor, not ketchup itself. Too loose for a hook.
- Exact origin region (Vietnam vs southern China): sources disagree, so the script says "dans le sud de la Chine", which both support for the name kê-tsiap.
