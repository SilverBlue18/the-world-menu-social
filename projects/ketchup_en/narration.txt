Your ketchup started life as a Chinese fish sauce.
In southern China, people fermented fish into a salty sauce called kê-tsiap. Around 1700, English merchants tasted it in Asia, and wanted it back home.
But they didn't have the fish. So they faked it. Mushroom ketchup. Walnut ketchup. Even oyster ketchup, with a hundred oysters.
Then in 1812, a Philadelphia scientist tried tomatoes. He called them love apples. Decades later, Heinz put it in a bottle.
The fish disappeared. Only the Chinese name survived. So every time you say ketchup, you're speaking a little Chinese.
