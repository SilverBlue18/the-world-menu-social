Fish, not tomato.
Your ketchup started life as a Chinese fish sauce. In southern China, they called it kê-tsiap.
Around 1700, English merchants got hooked on it in Asia. But back home, there was no such fish. So they improvised: mushrooms, walnuts, oysters.
Tomato only showed up in 1812, in an American recipe. And the bottled stuff? A chef of the time called it filthy and putrid.
Then came Henry Heinz. Thirty-one years old, and bankrupt. He started over with ketchup. In clear glass, so everyone could see exactly what was inside.
By 1905, Heinz had sold five million bottles. The fish is gone. But its name is still on your table.
